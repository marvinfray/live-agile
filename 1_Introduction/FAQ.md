
# Frequently Asked Questions

## Getting Started

### I'm new to Live Agile. Where do I start?
Start here:
1. Read [Welcome](Welcome.md) to understand the philosophy
2. Understand the [Core Components](../2_Framework_Overview/Core_Components.md) (The Wake, The Surface, The Current, The Horizon)
3. Review the [Season Plan template](../4_Templates/Template_Season_Plan.md)
4. Complete your first [Weekly Review](../5_Practices_and_Ceremonies/Weekly_Review.md)
5. Choose 3–5 movements for the coming week

### Can I practice Live Agile solo, or do I need a team?
Live Agile works beautifully both ways:
- **Solo practice**: Many practitioners use it for personal direction and reflection
- **Team practice**: Teams can practice together using shared Season Plans and Weekly Reviews
- **Hybrid**: You can maintain a personal practice while also participating in team cycles

Start wherever feels most natural. The framework scales to your context.

### Do I need any special tools or materials?
The essentials:
- A way to capture reflections (journal, document, or notes app)
- The [Season Plan template](../4_Templates/Template_Season_Plan.md)
- 30–60 minutes weekly for your Weekly Review
- A quiet space for reflection

Optional:
- The [Nested Goal Tracker](../tools/1077%20-%20Nested%20Goal%20Tracker%20%5BTemplate%5D.xlsx) for tracking outcomes over time
- A shared workspace if practicing with a team

## Framework & Philosophy

### Is Live Agile a productivity system?
No. Live Agile is a **presence system**.

It helps you:
- Stay aligned with what matters
- Navigate complexity with awareness
- Make sustainable choices
- Integrate learning and adaptation

It's about moving intentionally, not moving faster.

### How does Live Agile relate to traditional Agile/Scrum?
Live Agile reimagines agile principles through a lens of presence and human sustainability:
- Traditional Agile focuses on velocity, sprints, and delivery
- Live Agile focuses on awareness, rhythm, and meaningful movement

It draws from agile's iterative nature but centers humanity over productivity.

### What's the relationship between "Domain Language" and this framework?
The **Domain Language** (in the wiki folder) represents an earlier iteration that was more team-focused and used terminology closer to Scrum (Vision Keeper, Team Guide, Goal Setting, etc.).

The **current framework** (in the numbered sections) is the evolved version:
- More applicable to both individuals and teams
- Uses presence-oriented language (The Wake, The Surface, The Current, The Horizon)
- Emphasizes awareness and integration over roles and ceremonies

**For new practitioners**: Use the current framework (numbered sections). The Domain Language is preserved for context but is being phased out.

## Seasons & Timing

### Why 10–13 weeks specifically?
This timeframe creates meaningful rhythm:
- **Long enough** to accomplish substantial movement and see patterns emerge
- **Short enough** to maintain focus and adapt when needed
- **Aligned** with quarterly cycles (roughly 3 months) used in many organizational contexts

The 3-week flexibility (10–13) allows you to align with your context:
- Align with calendar quarters
- Start mid-month if needed
- Adjust around holidays or transitions

### Can I adjust the season length?
Yes, with intention.

**Shorter (8–10 weeks)**: Works well for fast-moving contexts or when starting out.
**Longer (13–16 weeks)**: Can work for slower-moving contexts, but watch for:
- Loss of focus
- Disconnection from the Horizon
- Reduced responsiveness

The key is maintaining regular Weekly Reviews regardless of season length.

### Do seasons align with calendar quarters (Q1, Q2, etc.)?
They can, but don't have to.

Many practitioners find it helpful to align with quarters for:
- Organizational reporting
- Financial cycles
- Team coordination

Others prefer to start seasons based on:
- Personal transitions
- Project milestones
- Natural energy cycles

Choose what serves your context.

## The Current (Weekly Movements)

### What exactly is a "movement"?
A movement is a meaningful unit of progress toward your outcomes.

**Not** a task, but a **direction of effort** that might include multiple tasks.

Examples:
- ✅ "Establish foundation for user authentication"
- ✅ "Clarify visual identity through exploration"
- ✅ "Strengthen relationship with key stakeholder"
- ❌ "Write 3 tests" (too granular)
- ❌ "Launch the product" (too large)

Think: **What meaningful shift can happen this week?**

### Why 3–5 movements specifically?
This constraint creates clarity:
- **Fewer than 3**: You might be thinking too broadly or avoiding focus
- **More than 5**: You're likely fragmenting attention or listing tasks instead of movements

The constraint helps you:
- Choose what truly matters
- Maintain sustainable pace
- Stay present to each movement

### How granular should movements be?
Each movement should be:
- **Completable** (or significantly advanced) within one week
- **Meaningful** in relation to your Season Plan outcomes
- **Observable** (you'll know if it happened)

If a movement feels too large, break it into multiple weekly movements.
If it feels too small, it might be a task within a larger movement.

## Weekly Practice

### How long should the Weekly Review take?
**Typical range**: 30–60 minutes

**Factors that influence duration**:
- Solo (30–45 min) vs. team (45–90 min)
- Depth of reflection
- Complexity of the week
- Whether you're reviewing multiple domains

Start with 30 minutes and adjust based on what serves you.

### When should I do my Weekly Review?
**Common patterns**:
- **Friday afternoon**: Close the work week with reflection
- **Sunday evening**: Prepare for the week ahead
- **Monday morning**: Set intention before engaging

**Teams**: Schedule a consistent time that works for everyone.

The key is consistency—same day/time each week builds rhythm.

### What if I miss a Weekly Review?
It happens. Here's how to reconnect:
1. **Don't try to recreate the missed week**—that moment has passed
2. **Acknowledge the gap**: "I lost connection with my rhythm"
3. **Start fresh**: Do a brief review of the last 2 weeks at a high level
4. **Choose movements** for the week ahead
5. **Resume rhythm** going forward

Missing one week doesn't break the practice. Missing three weeks means you've lost connection—restart your season.

## Daily Practice

### Is there a daily practice?
While Live Agile emphasizes the weekly rhythm, daily awareness supports the practice:

**The Surface Check-in** (5 minutes):
- What's my capacity today?
- What's the emotional weather?
- Which movement(s) will I advance today?
- What needs to shift?

This isn't a formal ceremony—it's a brief moment of grounding.

Some practitioners:
- Journal for 5–10 minutes each morning
- Use a daily standup format with teams
- Simply pause and breathe before starting work

Find what keeps you connected to The Surface.

### How do daily, weekly, and seasonal practices connect?
**Daily (The Surface)**: Present-moment awareness
- *"What's true right now?"*

**Weekly (The Current)**: Near-term movement
- *"What's alive this week? What did I learn?"*

**Seasonal (The Horizon)**: Directional intention
- *"Where am I heading? What story is unfolding?"*

Each zoom level informs the others:
- Daily awareness shapes weekly choices
- Weekly learning updates seasonal direction
- Seasonal intention guides daily focus

## Season Planning

### How many outcomes should a Season Plan have?
**Typical range**: 3–7 outcomes

**Guidelines**:
- **1–2 outcomes**: Intense focus (common during critical projects or transitions)
- **3–5 outcomes**: Balanced (most common)
- **6–8 outcomes**: Complex but manageable (requires strong prioritization)
- **9+ outcomes**: You're likely overwhelmed—consider focusing or splitting into multiple domains

Quality over quantity. Each outcome should matter.

### What are "Story Threads"?
Story Threads are the ongoing narratives that run through your season.

They're different from outcomes:
- **Outcomes** = destinations ("Launch v2.0", "Establish partnerships")
- **Story Threads** = ongoing movements ("Building technical foundation", "Deepening team trust")

A single Story Thread might contribute to multiple outcomes.
A single outcome might be supported by multiple Story Threads.

Think of them as the **currents** running through your season.

### Can you show an example of a filled-out Season Plan?
Example Season Plans are being developed. In the meantime:
- Use the [Season Plan template](../4_Templates/Template_Season_Plan.md) as your starting point
- Reference the guidance in [Season Plan (Artefact)](../3_Artefacts/Season_Plan.md)
- Start with 3–5 outcomes that matter to you this season

### What makes good "Boundaries"?
Boundaries define what you're intentionally **not** doing this season.

**Good boundaries are**:
- Specific: "No new feature requests outside the roadmap"
- Tempting: "No speaking engagements" (if you tend to say yes)
- Protective: "No weekend work except emergencies"

**Examples**:
- "I will not take on additional client projects"
- "We will not refactor legacy code this season"
- "I will not commit to new committee work"

Boundaries protect your Horizon from drift.

## Team Use

### How do teams use Live Agile together?
Teams can practice Live Agile at multiple levels:

**Shared Season Plan**: The team defines collective outcomes, Story Threads, and boundaries.

**Shared Weekly Reviews**: The team reflects together on what moved, what was learned, and what to carry forward.

**Individual Current**: Each team member defines their own 3–5 movements that contribute to team outcomes.

See [Applying Live Agile in Teams](../6_Scaling_and_Team_Use/Applying_Live_Agile_in_Teams.md) for detailed guidance.

### Can I use traditional Agile terminology alongside Live Agile?
Yes, many teams do this during transition:
- **"The Horizon"** ≈ Season Planning / Quarterly Planning
- **"The Current"** ≈ Sprint Backlog / Weekly Focus
- **"The Wake"** ≈ Retrospective / Review
- **"Outcomes"** ≈ OKRs / Goals

Over time, most teams find the Live Agile language more resonant—but use what serves your context.

### What roles exist in Live Agile teams?
Live Agile emphasizes **facilitation over fixed roles**:
- Someone holds the **Season Keeper** energy (maintaining the Horizon)
- Someone facilitates **Weekly Reviews** (can rotate)
- Team members share responsibility for movement

See the [Domain Language](../wiki/Domain%20Language.md) for role archetypes if your context requires them, but the framework works without formal roles.

## Troubleshooting

### I keep missing my movements. What's wrong?
This is feedback, not failure. Ask:
1. **Are my movements actually aligned with my outcomes?** (Misalignment drains motivation)
2. **Am I choosing too many movements?** (Try 3 instead of 5)
3. **Am I being honest about The Surface?** (Capacity, energy, constraints)
4. **Is my Season Plan still true?** (It might need updating)

The system reveals truth. Listen to what it's showing you.

### My Season Plan feels stale halfway through. Should I restart?
You have options:
1. **Refresh**: Update your outcomes and Story Threads while keeping the season arc
2. **Pivot**: Acknowledge the shift and start a new season
3. **Complete**: Finish the season with adjusted expectations, then plan the next one

There's no "right" answer—choose based on what serves learning and momentum.

### How do I handle unexpected urgent work?
Live Agile is designed for reality, which includes the unexpected:

**During Weekly Review**:
- Acknowledge what interrupted the plan
- Notice the impact on your movements
- Adjust The Current for the coming week

**Mid-week**:
- If urgency consumes the week, let it—your Weekly Review will capture this
- If possible, deprioritize one of your chosen movements to make space

The practice is about awareness, not perfect adherence.
