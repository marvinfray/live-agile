# Applying Live Agile in Teams

Live Agile works powerfully with teams of 2–7 people. This guide shows you how to practice together while maintaining individual agency.

---

## Team Practice Models

### Model 1: Shared Season, Individual Movements
**Best for**: Product teams, small startups, collaborative projects

**Structure**:
- **Team Season Plan**: Shared outcomes, Story Threads, and boundaries
- **Team Weekly Reviews**: Reflect together, share insights
- **Individual Movements**: Each person chooses their own 3–5 movements aligned with team outcomes

**Example**:
- **Team Outcome**: "Launch MVP of user dashboard"
- **Alice's movements**: "Complete API integration", "Establish error handling patterns"
- **Bob's movements**: "Finalize dashboard components", "Complete user testing"

### Model 2: Individual Seasons, Team Coordination
**Best for**: Cross-functional teams, agencies, leadership teams

**Structure**:
- **Individual Season Plans**: Each person has their own Season Plan
- **Team Weekly Sync**: Brief coordination (30 min) to share movements and dependencies
- **Individual Weekly Reviews**: Each person does their own review

**Example**:
- **Alice (Designer)**: Season focused on establishing design system
- **Bob (Engineer)**: Season focused on infrastructure improvements
- **Team Sync**: They coordinate where their work overlaps

### Model 3: Hybrid (Recommended for Most Teams)
**Best for**: Most team contexts

**Structure**:
- **Team Season Plan**: High-level direction and shared outcomes
- **Individual Season Components**: Each person adds their own outcomes/boundaries
- **Team Weekly Reviews**: Shared reflection (45–60 min)
- **Individual Weekly Reviews**: Optional additional solo reflection

## How to Start Team Practice

### Week 1: Individual Prep
Each team member:
1. Read [Getting Started](../1_Introduction/Getting_Started.md)
2. Complete an individual Weekly Review
3. Come with initial thoughts on team outcomes

### Week 2: First Team Season Planning (90–120 min)

**Facilitator** (one team member or external):
1. **Arrive** (10 min): Ground the team, set intention
2. **Context** (15 min): What's happening in the team/organization right now?
3. **Season Narrative** (20 min): What's the story of this season?
4. **Outcomes** (30 min): What 3–7 outcomes matter?
5. **Story Threads** (20 min): What ongoing movements run through them?
6. **Boundaries** (20 min): What will the team NOT do?
7. **Close** (10 min): Confirm the plan, establish weekly rhythm

**Output**: Shared Season Plan document

### Week 3+: Weekly Team Reviews

Follow the [Weekly Review](../5_Practices_and_Ceremonies/Weekly_Review.md) structure adapted for teams.

## Team Weekly Review Structure (45–90 min)

### For Teams of 3–5 People (45–60 min)

**1. Arrive & Breathe** (5 min)
- Gather (physical or virtual)
- Each person shares one word about their week
- Collective breath or moment of silence

**2. Review the Wake** (15 min)
- Each person shares 2 minutes on their week (what happened, not what was planned)
- Team listens without fixing
- Notice themes across the team

**3. Honour What Moved** (5 min)
- Each person names 1–2 things that moved forward
- Team acknowledges

**4. Acknowledge What Resisted** (5 min)
- Each person names what resisted
- No problem-solving yet—just witnessing

**5. Capture Insights** (10 min)
- Each person shares one key learning
- Team notices connections between insights
- Capture themes for future reference

**6. Release & Carry-Forward** (5 min)
- Anyone releasing commitments or Story Threads?
- Team supports decisions

**7. Choose Movements** (15 min)
- Each person shares their 3–5 movements for next week
- Team checks: "Does this feel aligned and achievable?"
- Note dependencies or collaboration opportunities

## Roles & Facilitation

### Do we need formal roles?
**Short answer**: No, but someone needs to hold certain energies.

**Team energies to hold**:

**Season Keeper**
- Maintains the Season Plan (keeps it visible and updated)
- Brings it into weekly reviews
- Notices when it needs refreshing

**Facilitator** (can rotate)
- Guides Weekly Reviews
- Holds the rhythm and structure
- Asks questions, doesn't direct

**Timekeeper**
- Watches timing during meetings
- Gently moves the team through sections

These can be shared or rotated. Avoid making them rigid roles.

## Shared Artefacts

### Team Season Plan
**Location**: Shared doc accessible to all team members

**Contains**:
- Context (team/org situation)
- Season Narrative (the story of this season)
- Outcomes (3–7 team outcomes)
- Story Threads (ongoing team movements)
- Boundaries (what the team won't do)

**Update frequency**: Check during Weekly Review, update mid-season if needed

**Example tools**: Google Doc, Notion, Confluence, shared markdown file

### Team Movement Board (Optional)
Some teams find it helpful to visualize movements:

**Simple format**:
```
Team Season Plan: Q1 2025

Current Week Movements:
- Alice: [3-5 movements]
- Bob: [3-5 movements]
- Carol: [3-5 movements]

Team Outcomes:
- Outcome 1: [Status, insights]
- Outcome 2: [Status, insights]
```

**Tools**: Shared doc, Trello, Notion, Miro board

## Team Patterns & Rhythms

### Weekly Team Rhythm
**Monday morning** (or team's choice):
- Team Weekly Review (45–60 min)
- Align on movements for the week
- Note dependencies

**Mid-week** (optional):
- Brief async check-in
- Share progress or blockers

**Friday** (optional):
- Async reflection or quick sync
- Celebrate what moved

### Seasonal Team Rhythm
**Season Start** (Week 1):
- Team Season Planning session (90–120 min)

**Mid-Season** (Week 6–7):
- Season Check-In (60 min)
- Ask: "Is our Season Plan still true?"
- Adjust if needed

**Season End** (Final week):
- Season Retrospective (60–90 min)
- Review: What did we learn?
- Plan: What's next season about?

## Common Team Challenges

### Challenge: "Our team rushes through reflection"
**Solutions**:
- Add more silence (2–3 min individual reflection before sharing)
- Use a timer for each section
- Assign a facilitator who slows the pace
- Remind: this is a presence practice, not a status meeting

### Challenge: "One person dominates the conversation"
**Solutions**:
- Use a talking token (only speaker holds it)
- Set time limits per person (e.g., 2 min each)
- Facilitator gently interrupts: "Thank you, let's hear from others"

### Challenge: "We skip Weekly Reviews when we're busy"
**Solutions**:
- Shorter review (20 min quick version) better than skipping
- Calendar block and treat as sacred
- Notice: when are we "too busy" to reflect?
- Ask: What's the resistance teaching us?

### Challenge: "Our Season Plan feels disconnected from real work"
**Solutions**:
- Review it every weekly meeting (don't hide it)
- Ask: "Does this still reflect reality?"
- Update it—it's meant to evolve
- Use movements to connect weekly work to seasonal outcomes

### Challenge: "Team members have conflicting priorities"
**Solutions**:
- Surface this in Season Planning—name the tension
- Establish clear boundaries ("We're focusing on X, not Y this season")
- Accept that some seasons have tension—that's information
- Consider whether everyone needs to be on the same Season Plan

## Team Size Adaptations

### 2–3 People (Small Team)
- Weekly Reviews: 30–45 min
- More intimate, can go deeper
- Easier to align on shared outcomes
- Consider: Do you need separate individual and team plans, or just one shared plan?

### 4–7 People (Standard Team)
- Weekly Reviews: 45–75 min
- Balance between individual voice and team coherence
- Need clear facilitation
- Consider: Hybrid model (shared + individual seasons)

### 8+ People (Large Team)
- Consider splitting into smaller teams
- Or: Use the team review for high-level alignment, smaller groups for detailed reflection
- Weekly Reviews: 60–90 min or break into sub-teams

## Async & Distributed Teams

### Full Async Model
**Monday**:
- Each person posts their Weekly Review in shared doc
- Format: Wake, Honour, Acknowledge, Insights, Movements for next week

**Tuesday**:
- Team reads each other's reviews
- Responds with acknowledgment or support
- Notes dependencies

**Wednesday** (optional):
- 30 min sync call for coordination if needed

**Friday**:
- Brief async shares: "What moved this week?"

### Hybrid Async/Sync
**Async** (Monday):
- Each person does individual reflection and posts movements

**Sync** (Tuesday, 30 min):
- Brief call to share insights
- Coordinate dependencies
- Acknowledge what moved/resisted

## Integrating with Existing Practices

### If your team already uses Scrum/Agile:

| Traditional Practice | Live Agile Adaptation |
|---------------------|----------------------|
| Sprint Planning | Becomes part of Weekly Review (choosing movements) |
| Daily Standup | Optional Daily Practice (Surface check-in) |
| Sprint Review | Integrated into Weekly Review (reviewing The Wake) |
| Retrospective | Integrated into Weekly Review (insights and learning) |
| Quarterly Planning | Becomes Season Planning |

**You can run Live Agile alongside traditional practices**, using Live Agile for the human/presence layer and traditional practices for delivery tracking.

### If your team uses OKRs:

**OKRs** can map to **Outcomes** in your Season Plan:
- OKRs often span quarters (≈ seasons)
- Outcomes are held more lightly in Live Agile
- Key Results can become Story Threads or Movements

**Integration**:
- Season Plan Outcomes align with OKRs
- Weekly Movements advance Key Results
- Weekly Reviews track progress and learning

## Starting a Team Practice: 4-Week Onboarding

### Week 1: Individual Foundation
- Each person reads [Getting Started](../1_Introduction/Getting_Started.md)
- Each person does an individual Weekly Review
- Team shares: "What resonated? What felt hard?"

### Week 2: First Team Season Planning
- 90–120 min facilitated session
- Create shared Season Plan
- Establish weekly rhythm (when, where, who facilitates)

### Week 3: First Team Weekly Review
- Follow full structure (allow 60–75 min)
- Debrief afterward: "How did that feel?"
- Adjust for next week

### Week 4: Refine & Commit
- Second Weekly Review (should feel more natural)
- Ask: "What's working? What needs to change?"
- Commit to the rhythm for the rest of the season

## Sample Team Season Plan

**Team**: Product team (4 people: 2 engineers, 1 designer, 1 PM)
**Season**: Q1 2025 (13 weeks)

### Context
We're a growing product team. Our user base doubled last quarter, and we're seeing performance issues. The company is pushing for new features, but our infrastructure is shaky. Team morale is good but we're feeling the pressure.

### Season Narrative
This season is about strengthening foundation while delivering selectively. We're saying no to feature bloat and yes to infrastructure resilience. By the end of this season, we should feel confident in our platform and clear on our roadmap.

### Outcomes
1. API response times under 200ms for 95th percentile
2. Automated deployment pipeline with rollback capability
3. Clear Q2 product roadmap with stakeholder alignment
4. Team operating principles documented and practiced

### Story Threads
- Building infrastructure resilience
- Clarifying product strategy
- Developing team practices
- Managing stakeholder expectations

### Boundaries
- No new feature development outside roadmap
- No weekend deployments except emergencies
- No skipping Weekly Reviews
