
# Season Planning Practice

Season Planning creates your Horizon—a 10–13 week arc of intention that guides without constraining.

---

## Overview

**Duration**: 60–120 minutes
- Solo practice: 60–90 minutes
- Team practice: 90–120 minutes

**Frequency**: Once per season (every 10–13 weeks)
- Typically aligned with quarters (Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4)
- Or timed around transitions, projects, or life changes

**Context**: Solo or team
- **Solo**: Personal reflection and direction-setting
- **Team**: Shared direction and collective commitment

---

## When to Do Season Planning

### Start of a Season
- Beginning of a quarter
- Start of a new year
- Beginning of a project or initiative

### Mid-Season (if needed)
- When the original Season Plan no longer reflects reality
- After a major organizational or life change
- When you've lost connection with The Horizon

### End of Previous Season
- Final week of current season
- Reflects on learnings
- Transitions into next season

---

## Materials Needed

### Essential
- Quiet, uninterrupted space (90–120 min block)
- Blank Season Plan template ([Template](../4_Templates/Template_Season_Plan.md))
- Way to capture notes (doc, journal, whiteboard)
- Previous Season Plan (if you have one)

### Helpful
- Calendar (to confirm season dates)
- Previous 2–3 weeks of Weekly Reviews (for context)
- Organizational goals or team OKRs (if relevant)
- Facilitator (for teams or first-time solo practitioners)

## Solo Season Planning (60–90 min)

### Preparation (before the session)
- Block 90 minutes of uninterrupted time
- Review your last 2–3 Weekly Reviews
- If you have a previous Season Plan, review it
- Come with curiosity, not answers

---

### Step 1: Arrive & Ground (5–10 min)

**Purpose**: Transition into reflective mode.

**Practice**:
- Close distractions (phone, notifications, other apps)
- Take 5 deep breaths
- Light a candle, make tea, or create a small ritual
- Set intention: "I'm here to see what wants to emerge"

**Question to hold**:
> "What season is this?"

---

### Step 2: Review The Wake (15–20 min)

**Purpose**: Learn from the previous season (if applicable).

**If you have a previous Season Plan**:
- Read it with curiosity
- What actually happened?
- Which outcomes were achieved? Which weren't?
- What Story Threads emerged?
- What did you learn about yourself, your work, your capacity?

**Questions**:
- What patterns do I notice?
- What surprised me?
- What did this season teach me?

**If this is your first Season Plan**:
- Review the past 3 months
- What's been alive in your work/life?
- What's been taking your energy?
- What patterns do you notice?

**Capture**: Write 3–5 key learnings from the past season/period.

---

### Step 3: Name the Context (10 min)

**Purpose**: Ground the Season Plan in current reality.

**Questions**:
- What's happening in my life/work right now?
- What external factors are shaping this season?
- What's my capacity? (physical, mental, emotional, contextual)
- What's the current reality?

**Examples**:
> "New role, learning to lead a team. Excited but anxious. Stable personal life. Medium-high capacity."

> "Launching a new product in Q1. Team is small but aligned. Org is going through restructuring—some uncertainty."

**Capture**: Write 2–4 sentences describing the context of this season.

---

### Step 4: Craft the Season Narrative (15–20 min)

**Purpose**: Tell the story of this season.

This is the most important step—it creates coherence.

**Questions**:
- What's the story of this season?
- What wants to unfold over the next 10–13 weeks?
- What's the through-line?
- What's the feeling or energy of this season?

**Prompts**:
- "This season is about..."
- "By the end of this season, I want to feel..."
- "The question I'm living into this season is..."

**Examples**:
> "This season is about establishing foundation. After 6 months of rapid growth, I need to strengthen my systems, clarify my direction, and create space to breathe."

> "This is a season of exploration and learning. I'm stepping into a new domain, and I don't need to have all the answers—I need to be curious."

**Avoid**:
- ❌ Lists of tasks
- ❌ Generic statements ("work hard", "be productive")
- ❌ Should statements ("I should be more...")

**Capture**: Write 2–4 sentences that capture the narrative of this season.

---

### Step 5: Define Outcomes (20–25 min)

**Purpose**: Name 3–7 meaningful destinations.

**Questions**:
- What outcomes matter this season?
- What would make this season feel meaningful?
- What aligns with the narrative?

**Guidelines**:
- **3–7 outcomes** (not more—focus matters)
- Each outcome should be:
  - **Meaningful** (not trivial)
  - **Observable** (you'll know if it happened)
  - **Seasonal** (achievable in 10–13 weeks)
- Outcomes can be professional, personal, or mixed

**Examples**:
- ✅ "Establish sustainable leadership practices"
- ✅ "Launch MVP of customer dashboard"
- ✅ "Build morning reflection practice"
- ❌ "Be a better person" (not observable)
- ❌ "Write 10,000 lines of code" (not meaningful)

**Test each outcome**: Does this align with my Season Narrative?

**Capture**: List 3–7 outcomes. For each, write 1 sentence on what success looks like.

---

### Step 6: Identify Story Threads (15 min)

**Purpose**: Name the ongoing movements that run through the season.

Story Threads are different from Outcomes—they're the currents, not the destinations.

**Questions**:
- What ongoing movements will support my outcomes?
- What themes connect my outcomes?
- What practices or capacities am I developing?

**Examples**:
- "Building technical foundation"
- "Strengthening stakeholder relationships"
- "Developing strategic thinking"
- "Cultivating team trust"

**Guidelines**:
- 3–6 Story Threads
- A single Story Thread can support multiple outcomes
- Think: What's the ongoing work that matters?

**Capture**: Create a table:

| Story Thread | Description | Linked Outcomes |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Building leadership capacity | Learning to delegate, give feedback, hold boundaries | Outcomes 2, 4 |

---

### Step 7: Establish Boundaries (10–15 min)

**Purpose**: Name what you will NOT do this season.

Boundaries protect your Season Plan from drift and overcommitment.

**Questions**:
- What will I intentionally NOT do this season?
- What temptations do I need to decline?
- What would dilute my focus?

**Guidelines**:
- Be specific (not "work less", but "no weekend work except emergencies")
- Name real temptations (things you'd actually be tempted to do)
- 3–5 boundaries

**Examples**:
- "No new projects outside current commitments"
- "No speaking engagements this season"
- "No skipping Weekly Reviews"
- "No taking on additional committee work"

**Capture**: List 3–5 specific boundaries.

---

### Step 8: Close & Commit (5 min)

**Purpose**: Honor the plan and commit to the rhythm.

**Reflect**:
- Read your full Season Plan
- Does it feel true?
- Does it feel aligned?
- Is it realistic given your capacity (The Surface)?

**Commit**:
- When will you do your Weekly Reviews?
- Where will you keep your Season Plan visible?
- When will you check in mid-season?

**Capture**: Write your commitment.

## Team Season Planning (90–120 min)

### Preparation (before the session)

**Each team member**:
- Read [Getting Started](../1_Introduction/Getting_Started.md)
- Complete an individual Weekly Review
- Come with initial thoughts on team outcomes

**Facilitator**:
- Book 90–120 min session
- Prepare shared document for Season Plan
- Review team's previous Season Plan (if exists)

---

## Full Facilitator Script (Team Season Planning)

### Opening & Arrive (10 min)

**Facilitator**:
> "Welcome. We're here to create our Season Plan—the story and direction for the next 10–13 weeks.
>
> This isn't about committing to a rigid plan. It's about naming what matters, creating coherence, and establishing boundaries.
>
> We'll spend the next 90–120 minutes together. We're looking for alignment, not perfection.
>
> Let's arrive. Close what you don't need open. Take a breath together.
>
> [30 seconds of silence]
>
> Let's begin."

---

### Step 1: Review The Wake (Team Context) (15 min)

**Facilitator**:
> "If we have a previous Season Plan, let's look at it together. Not to judge ourselves, but to learn.
>
> [If applicable: Display previous Season Plan]
>
> Take 2 minutes individually: What do you notice? What did we achieve? What surprised us?
>
> [2 minutes silence]
>
> Now let's hear from each person. 1–2 minutes: What stands out to you from the last season?"

**[Each person shares]**

**Facilitator captures themes on shared doc.**

---

### Step 2: Name the Context (15 min)

**Facilitator**:
> "What's happening for our team right now? What's the context we're operating in?
>
> Let's consider:
> - What's happening in the organization?
> - What's the team capacity?
> - What external pressures or opportunities exist?
> - What's the current reality?
>
> Take 2 minutes to reflect individually.
>
> [2 minutes silence]
>
> Let's go around. What's one piece of context that feels important?"

**[Each person shares]**

**Facilitator synthesizes into 3–4 sentences:**
> "Here's what I'm hearing... [reads back]. Does this capture it? Anything to add?"

**Capture in Season Plan document.**

---

### Step 3: Craft Season Narrative (20 min)

**Facilitator**:
> "Now, the story. What's this season about?
>
> Not a list of tasks—but a narrative. A through-line.
>
> Prompts to consider:
> - 'This season is about...'
> - 'By the end of this season, we want to feel...'
> - 'The question we're living into is...'
>
> Take 3 minutes individually to draft a narrative.
>
> [3 minutes silence]
>
> Let's share. Read what you wrote."

**[Each person shares their draft]**

**Facilitator synthesizes**:
> "I'm hearing themes of [X, Y, Z]. Let's try to weave this together."

**[Facilitator drafts narrative collaboratively with team, iterating until it feels true]**

**Final check**:
> "Does this narrative feel aligned? True? Anything to adjust?"

**Capture in Season Plan document.**

---

### Step 4: Define Outcomes (30 min)

**Facilitator**:
> "What outcomes matter this season? What would make this season meaningful?
>
> We're looking for 3–7 outcomes. They should:
> - Align with our narrative
> - Be observable (we'll know if they happened)
> - Be achievable in 10–13 weeks
>
> Take 5 minutes individually to draft outcomes.
>
> [5 minutes silence—each person writes]
>
> Let's share. Go around and read your outcomes."

**[Each person shares]**

**Facilitator**:
> "Now let's find the overlap and prioritize. Which outcomes feel most essential?"

**[Team discussion: 15 minutes]**
- Look for common themes
- Combine similar outcomes
- Prioritize ruthlessly
- Aim for 3–7 final outcomes

**Facilitator**:
> "Here's what I'm hearing as our 3–7 outcomes... [reads list]. Does this feel right?"

**Team adjusts until consensus.**

**Capture in Season Plan document with one sentence each on what success looks like.**

---

### Step 5: Identify Story Threads (15 min)

**Facilitator**:
> "Story Threads are the ongoing movements that run through our season. They connect our outcomes.
>
> Questions:
> - What ongoing work supports these outcomes?
> - What themes connect them?
> - What are we building over time?
>
> Take 2 minutes to think.
>
> [2 minutes silence]
>
> Let's brainstorm Story Threads together."

**[Team brainstorms, facilitator captures]**

**Facilitator helps organize**:
> "I'm seeing these themes... [lists 4–6 Story Threads]. Let's link them to outcomes."

**Create table collaboratively:**

| Story Thread | Description | Linked Outcomes |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|

**Capture in Season Plan document.**

---

### Step 6: Establish Boundaries (15 min)

**Facilitator**:
> "Boundaries protect our Season Plan. They name what we will NOT do.
>
> This takes courage. What will we say no to this season?
>
> Take 2 minutes to think about what would dilute our focus.
>
> [2 minutes silence]
>
> Let's share. What boundaries do we need?"

**[Each person shares]**

**Facilitator captures and tests each**:
> "Is this specific? Is it a real temptation? Will it protect our focus?"

**Final list: 3–5 team boundaries.**

**Examples**:
- "No new feature development outside this roadmap"
- "No weekend deployments except emergencies"
- "No skipping Weekly Reviews"

**Capture in Season Plan document.**

---

### Step 7: Close & Commit (10 min)

**Facilitator reads the full Season Plan**:
> "Here's our Season Plan for [dates]:
>
> **Context**: [reads]
> **Narrative**: [reads]
> **Outcomes**: [reads list]
> **Story Threads**: [reads]
> **Boundaries**: [reads]
>
> Does this feel true? Aligned? Realistic given our capacity?"

**Team reflects and adjusts if needed.**

**Facilitator**:
> "Final commitments:
> - When will we do Weekly Reviews? [Team decides]
> - Who will keep the Season Plan visible? [Assign Season Keeper]
> - When will we check in mid-season? [Set date for Week 6–7]
>
> Thank you for your presence. This is our Horizon. Let's trust it and let it evolve."

## Variations

### Quick Season Plan (30–45 min)
For experienced practitioners or time constraints:
1. **Context** (5 min): What's happening now?
2. **Narrative** (10 min): What's the story?
3. **Outcomes** (15 min): What 3–5 outcomes matter?
4. **Boundaries** (10 min): What won't we do?

Skip Story Threads if needed.

### Deep Season Plan (2–3 hours)
For transitions, major decisions, or complex contexts:
- Extended Wake review (30 min)
- Deeper context exploration (20 min)
- More time for narrative development (30 min)
- Detailed outcome definition with success criteria
- Multiple rounds of refinement

---

## Mid-Season Check-In (60 min)

At Week 6–7 of your season, do a check-in:

### Solo (30 min)
1. Re-read your Season Plan
2. Ask:
   - Is this still true?
   - What's working? What's not?
   - Do outcomes need to shift?
   - Are boundaries holding?
3. Update as needed

### Team (60 min)
1. Re-read Season Plan together
2. Each person shares:
   - What's working?
   - What feels misaligned?
   - What needs to shift?
3. Update outcomes, Story Threads, or boundaries if needed
4. Reconfirm commitment

## Common Questions

### "Our outcomes are vague. Is that okay?"
Some vagueness is natural—clarity emerges with movement. But each outcome should be observable enough that you'll know whether you achieved it.

**Test**: Could you tell someone at the end of the season: "Yes, we did this" or "No, we didn't"?

### "We have 10+ outcomes. Should we narrow it down?"
Yes. More than 7 outcomes usually means:
- You're listing tasks, not outcomes
- You're overcommitting
- You haven't prioritized

Choose what truly matters. Everything else goes in boundaries ("What we're NOT doing").

### "Can we change our Season Plan mid-season?"
Yes. The Season Plan should evolve with reality. If something significant changes (organizational shift, life transition, major learning), update it.

**Avoid**: Changing it every week because of minor resistance. Some friction is normal.

### "Should we align with organizational OKRs?"
If it serves you. Many teams map Season Plan Outcomes to OKRs. But don't sacrifice the narrative and presence-focus to fit rigid quarterly targets.

**Balance**: Organizational alignment + team/individual sustainability.

---

## Facilitator Principles

### 1. Hold Space, Don't Direct
Your role is to guide the process, not to decide outcomes. Ask questions, don't provide answers.

### 2. Slow Down
Teams often rush to outcomes. Spend time on narrative—it creates coherence.

### 3. Encourage Honesty
The best Season Plans name what's true, not what sounds good.

### 4. Protect Boundaries
Boundaries often get negotiated away. Hold firm: they protect the season.

### 5. Trust Emergence
You don't need to have the perfect plan by the end of the session. Clarity emerges with movement.
